Haarlem Hold'em TV Host

Setup
1. Connect the TV — open this page on your TV or tablet. It becomes the dealer.
2. Players join — scan the QR code (or open the link) on your own phone, and enter your name.
Tip: use your phone's own Camera app (or Google Lens) to scan the code, not a separate QR-scanner app — some of those show ad banners that can block taps lower on the screen.
3. Host starts — once everyone's seated, tap Start Game.
Host controls
Kick (seat list) — removes a specific player from their seat and blocks their phone from automatically rejoining that seat. Use this if the wrong person joined, someone had to leave, or a phone needs to be freed up for someone else. Kicks don't expire on their own — they stay in place until you lift them.
Clear All Bans — lifts every kick from the previous game, all at once, so anyone can rejoin.
Start New Game — clears every seat immediately, including a phone left open in the background, so the next game starts genuinely empty.
Quit Game — stops the current hand and returns to the lobby. Doesn't kick anyone.
Leaderboard — ranks every player by their current chip count, for this session only.
How to Play
Each player gets 2 hole cards, face-down, visible only on their own phone. Betting happens before the flop, then after the flop (3 shared cards appear), the turn (1 more), and the river (1 more). Best 5-card hand wins.
To view your own cards, press and drag upward on them — like squeezing real cards to peek at the corner. Let go and they lie flat again. Nobody else can ever see them.
Check/Fold & Sit Out
Two checkboxes sit below your action buttons, always available:
Check/Fold — arm this and the game checks for you if nobody's bet, or folds if someone has, the instant your turn arrives. Re-arms fresh every street, so it can never act on cards you haven't seen yet. Uncheck it any time before your turn resolves to act normally instead.
Sit Out — you're still dealt real cards every hand, but automatically fold the moment it's your turn — no need to wait for anything before stepping away. Stays on until you turn it off yourself, even across hands.
Chip colors on the table
Chips shown during betting have no number on them — the color and chip count show the amount. Each color is worth 4× the one below it, always relative to the CURRENT small blind (so this scales automatically as blinds go up):
Red = 1× small blind
Blue = 4× small blind
Green = 16× small blind
Black = 64× small blind
Purple = 256× small blind — the biggest bets and all-ins
Your exact stack and the amount to call are always shown on your own phone.
Hand ranking, best to worst:
1. Straight Flush — 5 cards in a row, all the same suit
10♦J♦Q♦K♦A♦ (a Royal Flush — the best possible straight flush)
Higher top card wins — a Jack-high straight flush beats a 9-high one.
2. Four of a Kind — 4 cards of the same rank
7♠7♥7♦7♣2♠
Higher rank wins — four Kings beats four Sevens.
3. Full House — 3 of a kind plus a pair
K♠K♥K♦4♣4♠
The three-of-a-kind rank decides it first — Kings-full-of-Fours beats Nines-full-of-Aces.
4. Flush — 5 cards, all the same suit, not in order
2♠6♠9♠J♠K♠
Compared card-by-card from highest down — the flush with the higher top card wins.
5. Straight — 5 cards in a row, mixed suits
4♥5♠6♦7♣8♠
Higher top card wins — an 8-high straight beats a 7-high one.
6. Three of a Kind — 3 cards of the same rank
9♠9♥9♦3♣K♠
Higher rank wins — three Nines beats three Fours.
7. Two Pair — 2 separate pairs
J♠J♥5♦5♣2♠
The higher pair decides it first, then the lower pair.
8. Pair — 2 cards of the same rank
Q♠Q♥7♦4♣2♠
Higher pair wins — a pair of Queens beats a pair of Sevens.
9. High Card — no combination, highest single card wins
A♠K♥9♦5♣2♠
If the top card ties, the next-highest card decides it, and so on.
Show or Muck
At showdown, you don't always have to reveal your cards. The player who bet or raised last has to show first — everyone after that gets to choose Show or Muck. Mucking hides your hand for good — even if it would have won, nobody ever sees it. Your secrets are safe, win or lose.
One exception: if a hand goes all the way to showdown with an all-in on the table, everyone shows — no mucking, per standard rules.
Blinds & Antes
Small and big blinds — plus a small ante every player puts in — increase automatically as the game goes on, with a countdown shown on the TV and on your phone. This keeps the game moving and forces decisions as stacks get tighter, standard for any real poker tournament.
Final Standings
If two or more players bust out in the very same hand, they still need a finishing order. Per industry standard: whoever had more chips at the start of that hand finishes higher; if that's also equal, the stronger hand at that showdown breaks the tie.
